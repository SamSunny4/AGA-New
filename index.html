About Kerala SafeRoute (Student Project)

Kerala SafeRoute is a student project demonstrating how fundamental Graph Data Structures and Algorithms can assist emergency planning authorities during monsoons and coastal inundation events in Kerala.

1. Graph Representation

  • Vertices $(V)$: Key road intersections, bridges, and designated emergency relief shelters in the Kochi urban corridor.
  • Edges $(E)$: Connecting roads with physical distance $(km)$ and topographical elevation $(m)$.

2. Simulated Risk Estimate & Edge Cost Formulation

Traditional navigation systems optimize purely for the shortest physical distance. In disaster scenarios, shorter paths often traverse flooded depressions or riverbanks. In this project:

Edge Weight $W_e = \text{Distance}_e \times \left(1 + 2.5 \times \frac{\text{SimulatedRisk}_e}{100}\right)$

Where $\text{SimulatedRisk}$ is calculated using a transparent heuristic:
• Elevation Penalty: Roads below 4 meters suffer higher risk multipliers.
• Hazard Proximity: Roads crossing the Periyar river floodplain or Vembanad backwater receive hazard weight surcharges.
• Severity Multiplier: Yellow (1.0x), Orange (1.8x), and Red Alert (3.0x).

3. Dijkstra's Algorithm

Dijkstra's algorithm finds the path that minimizes the cumulative combined travel and risk weight. When roads are marked closed, their edges are removed from the graph, forcing the algorithm to find alternative higher-ground routes.
